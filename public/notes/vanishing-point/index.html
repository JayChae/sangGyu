Vanishing Point

The vanishing point is both the origin of the laws of perspective that govern the pictorial field and the point at which representation comes to an end. It is where what the viewer sees becomes incomprehensible, and where the viewer, in turn, becomes the object of the gaze of the Real beyond the screen.
The fact that the vanishing point is visible paradoxically means that the infinity of the world corresponding to that point cannot be seen.
This gap—between beginning and end, seeing and being seen, the visible and the unseeable—reveals that although the subject constitutes the world they see, they can never see the world in its entirety. Something always remains.
