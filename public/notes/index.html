Artist's Notes

Park Sang Gyu 박상규

Basic Geometric Forms and Organic Forms

Basic geometric forms self-evidently reveal the principles by which they are constituted. Existing with excessive simplicity and clarity, they appear alien.
Organic forms have neither angles nor points. They offer no focal point at which the gaze can converge; instead, they continually deflect it. They are like vast vanishing points that endlessly redirect the gaze beyond the visible, toward what can no longer be seen.

Forms that can no longer be divided; forms that endlessly fragment.
Indivisible, lucid forms; forms that resist comprehension.
Hard, masculine materiality embodies the Constructivist tradition of sculptural form, in which a unified conception exists.
Softly curved forms and organic surfaces embody the encounter between the Real within sensory experience and the material world.

There are no abstract concepts that exist solely in thought. All metaphysical ideas are grounded in the material realities of the world, and the metaphysical originates in the physical.
Life and ideas cannot be separated.

Vanishing Point

The vanishing point is an intriguing subject. Borrowing from Lacan’s theory of the image-screen, it is the point where things that cannot be represented within the image begin to emerge. It is where the subject can no longer maintain a sovereign position in gazing at the screen, and where, instead, the Real beyond the screen gazes back at the subject. It is the point where the logic of perspective governing the work comes to an end, and where the invisible, the unspeakable, and that which cannot be grasped by reason begin to break through.

Repetition gives rise to patterns, and becoming immersed in the geometry of these patterns is an experience of overwhelming depth. It is an experience of repetitive patterns that simultaneously enables recognition and carries us beyond it. This process of experiencing contradictory patterns repeated toward the vanishing point may, amid the repetitions of everyday life, ultimately give rise to a longing to reach the vanishing point.

Roadkill

The experience of traveling in a car feels to me like a virtual experience within a virtual space. My body expands to the dimensions of the car I am driving, experiencing a disembodied sense of speed. As I move toward the fixed vanishing point far ahead on the road, everything immediately beside me changes rapidly from moment to moment, losing its continuity in my perception. The passing surroundings become fragmented, experienced as a succession of individual frames.

The geometric yet organic forms covered in fur are the carcasses of animals I have witnessed, fragmented and captured within a single frame. Through the abstracted information I belatedly recall from that striking frame in which a mass of fur appeared, I can know almost nothing about them. All I can perceive is that their bodies were curled into rounded forms and the color of their fur.

These balls of fur, already dead or perhaps approaching death, appeared to me like powerful negative impressions of life. Amid the repetition of similar landscapes, as I traveled toward the distant vanishing point, I encountered a sensation of life imprinted in negative within a single frame. To represent that sensation might be an act of translating what was experienced as a negative impression back into a positive relief.

Once, I struck a small bird that flew toward my car. The collision left a mark on the windshield. For me, who had been observing passing deaths almost voyeuristically from within the car, a position isolated from the outside world, this became an event in which death directly entered my own experience. Traumatic ideas, when connected to actual events, cease to remain isolated within the realm of fantasy and become firmly grounded in reality.

Oxidation

Rust blooms, and forms grow precariously upward. Rust is the corrosion and destruction of metal, yet also an event whose emergence is eagerly anticipated. A rusted surface is time compressed over many years, and this accumulated time is not a continuous present, but an interconnected past, present, and future.

Related works: Beyond the Predetermined, Open Lack – Oxidized Figures #10, #6, #5, #3, #2

Residual Sensation

Deconstruction proceeds through comprehension and analysis, yet complete deconstruction is impossible, for nothing can be perfectly defined. The redefinitions and recodings that emerge from the process of deconstruction give rise to new forms of power and new forms of oppression.
What we organize and comprehend through reason and logic, we do so through language. We understand, think, and communicate through language. Yet before analysis, before we perceive our sensations and translate them into words, there are feelings and sensations themselves. They resist deconstruction at the most fundamental level.
How can language, which derives from sensation, define, comprehend, and position itself above sensation?
These are what we call residues, sensations, and spirituality. They precede language, and therefore precede power; they neither reestablish power nor impose new codes. There is no violence within them. They simply exist, resisting deconstruction.

Once we have comprehended everything that can be comprehended, what remains is that which cannot be grasped.
The traumatic and the uncompromising. The residues of the Real that cannot be assimilated into the Symbolic. For Gerhard Richter, the blurred and shimmering surfaces. For Andy Warhol, death and disaster, torn and misaligned surfaces. Lacan’s vanishing point.

Related works: Beyond the Predetermined, Self-Portrait, A Face, Arhats – Little Beings, The Pool of Two Worlds, Combustion, Untitled

Irreducibility

Sensation is irreducible and contradictory. Yet if we regard these qualities as the limits of our own capacities, where rigorous analysis and rational effort are frustrated, or as an unknown oppressive force, we are attempting to place sensation somewhere removed from everyday life. To sense and enjoy the living world would then become something confined to the realm of fantasy.
Yet these sensations are significant, and they are all around us. Residues, sensations, and spirituality that precede logical conclusions are always already experienced in our everyday lives through their irreducible, contradictory, and ambivalent qualities.

Before we comprehend and define it, the world already exists. Indifferent to human logic, it appears contradictory and ambivalent to us. Remarkably, this mode of existence in the external world also characterizes our inner sensations.
A sense of fullness can coexist with sorrow and inner emptiness without eliminating either, while simultaneously filling our inner being. Likewise, an unrepresentable wholeness can be momentarily perceived by our fragmented and partial selves.
Human perception and choice impose logic, structure, and discipline upon these experiences, organizing them into a coherent understanding. Yet the process of comprehension and organization through language inevitably entails omissions and incompleteness.

Related works: Beyond the Predetermined, Self-Portrait, A Face, Collective Gaze, Brave New World, Ecology, Combustion, A Cushion

Analysis and Deconstruction

Questioning systems, structures, and disciplines allows us to reflect upon them through understanding and analysis, while simultaneously bringing about deconstruction.
Here, deconstruction may lead toward nihilism, or it may become a form of consolation that embraces the differences of those oppressed by disciplines established through logic and authority, thereby expanding the boundaries of our understanding.
In the Real that precedes language, in sensations before they are framed by language, both possibilities coexist.

Related works: Self-Portrait, Collective Gaze, The Stranger, Brave New World, Ecology, Untitled, A Cushion

Systems

We perceive the world through the lens of language. Language is logic, structure, and discipline. It is a system and a form of authority, and we are surrounded by these forces.
Questioning what drives our actions and the structures to which we belong is profoundly difficult. Nevertheless, the effort to question and understand remains necessary.
Questioning systems can lead to a broader understanding of both ourselves and those around us, while recognizing their incomprehensibility may bring compassion and reflection toward our own smaller, limited selves.

Related works: Self-Portrait, Collective Gaze, The Stranger, Arhats – Little Beings, Brave New World, Ecology
