Negativity and Identity

We can determine what an object is by distinguishing it from other things and drawing its boundaries. This act of determination involves negation: identifying what the object is not. Such negativity limits identity, and it is through this limitation that identity is constituted.

When we distinguish growth from destruction and presence from absence, we establish one meaning while placing the other in opposition to it. Yet the corrosion that destroys metal forms the surface of the work, and hollowed-out space constitutes form. Destruction and absence seem to impede formation and presence, yet at the same time make them possible.

Here, formation and destruction, presence and absence, are distinct yet inseparable. When we recognize one term, its opposite comes to mind at the same time.
